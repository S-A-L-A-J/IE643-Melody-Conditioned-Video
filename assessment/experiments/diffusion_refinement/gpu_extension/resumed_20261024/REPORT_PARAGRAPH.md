We initialized AnimateDiff refinement with videos predicted by our music-conditioned GRU and evaluated a small spatial appearance adapter trained for 128 updates on existing synthetic training data. At inference, we used six low-strength DDIM steps and averaged final latent predictions from matched positive and negative Gaussian noise. Across the first eight validation recordings, adapted paired refinement reduced target-latent MSE by 5.6% and decoded-image MSE by 3.6%; after freezing the method, all three remaining validation recordings also improved, with image MSE reduced by 4.4%. Estimated-beat F1 was unchanged. Fixed frame inspections showed modestly more defined ring edges, while missing target details remained. Frozen pretrained refinement did not provide a comparable image-error benefit. These are synthetic-domain validation findings, not independent test results or human quality ratings. Music conditioning remains in the GRU initialization: the diffusion adapter learns visual appearance, not direct audio conditioning, and estimated-beat metrics do not establish semantic or mood accuracy.

We also extended the preserved GRU to 8- and 16-second generation using rolling four-second context and applied diffusion through overlapping temporal windows at each global denoising step. On one validation recording, estimated-beat F1 remained 0.903 and 0.968 respectively after refinement; paired diffusion sampling took approximately 45 and 108 seconds. Wrong/shifted-audio controls changed only upstream predictor inputs, so they demonstrate inherited conditioning rather than audio learning within diffusion. Long-video generalization and target fidelity beyond four seconds were not established.
